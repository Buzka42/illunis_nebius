# Four kinds of exercise for older adults

MedlinePlus, drawing on the US National Institute on Aging, describes four kinds of activity and notes that doing all of them gives more benefit than any one alone.

1. **Endurance (aerobic)**: brisk walking or jogging, dancing, swimming, biking. These raise breathing and heart rate.
2. **Strength**: lifting weights or using a resistance band to build stronger muscles.
3. **Balance**: exercises that help prevent falls.
4. **Flexibility**: stretching that keeps the body limber.

If you have not been active, the guidance is to start slowly and work up to your goal. How much you need depends on age and health, and the page advises checking with a health provider about what suits you. The page also links to resources from the National Institute on Aging, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and the National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health, including information on tai chi and qigong, which combine gentle movement with balance and breathing.

Why the mix matters: balance and strength work are especially important with age because they help reduce falls and keep people independent, for example getting out of a chair or bed unaided.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: when a user is older, or caring for someone older, point to all four types as a menu, and start with whichever the person already enjoys. Avoid prescriptive programs. Falls, dizziness, joint pain or unexplained tiredness need a clinician, and the musculoskeletal and general medicine experts may add general information only.

# What regular activity is linked with

The NHS describes strong evidence that being active supports a healthier and happier life at any age. Regular activity is linked with a lower risk of:

- coronary heart disease and stroke,
- type 2 diabetes,
- bowel cancer, and breast cancer in women,
- osteoarthritis and hip fracture,
- falls in older adults,
- depression and dementia,
- early death. The page says risk of early death can be lowered by up to 30 percent.

It also reports that activity can improve self-esteem, mood, sleep quality and energy, and reduce the risk of stress. These are population-level associations. They do not predict outcomes for one person.

The "what counts" idea is simple. To benefit, you need to move fast enough to raise your heart rate, breathe faster and feel warmer. That is moderate intensity: you can talk, but not sing. Vigorous intensity, where you cannot say more than a few words, brings additional benefit. For most people the easiest route is to fold activity into daily life, for example walking or cycling instead of driving, and more is generally better than less, up to sensible limits.

The page points to separate guidelines for under-5s, children and young people, adults and older adults, so numbers differ by age.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: when a user is low on energy or mood, a short walk is a realistic, evidence-backed option to mention, framed as something that often helps rather than a fix. Keep the language about tendencies and choices. For low mood that persists, or any health condition, the mental health or general medicine expert and a clinician are the right next step.

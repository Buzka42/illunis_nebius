# Gentle flexibility work at home

The NHS offers a short set of flexibility exercises that can be done at home to support mobility. They are described as gentle and suitable even for people who have not exercised for a while. The advice is to wear loose, comfortable clothes, keep water nearby, build up slowly, and gradually increase repetitions over time. The suggestion is to do them at least twice a week and to combine them with the sitting, balance and strength routines in the same series.

Examples from the set, in general terms:

- **Neck rotation**: sit upright with shoulders relaxed, turn the head slowly toward one shoulder as far as is comfortable, hold briefly, return, and repeat on the other side. Three each way.
- **Neck stretch**: while holding one shoulder down, tilt the head slowly to the opposite side, hold about 5 seconds, three times per side.
- **Sideways bend**: standing with feet hip-width apart, slide one arm down the side of the body as far as is comfortable to feel a stretch along the opposite hip, hold a couple of seconds, three per side.
- **Calf stretch**: with hands on a wall, step one leg back, keep it straight with the heel on the floor, and feel the stretch in the calf. Three per side.

The movements are slow and held only briefly, and none should be painful. Comfort is the limit, not a target range.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: a short stretch break fits well after long periods of sitting, and pairs naturally with a calming moment in the day. If a stretch causes sharp pain, numbness or tingling, or an old injury flares, stop and take it to a clinician or physiotherapist; the musculoskeletal expert may add general context.

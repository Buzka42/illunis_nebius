# Walking as an easy way to be more active

Walking is described as simple, free and one of the easiest ways to be more active. A brisk walk can build stamina and support heart health, and you do not need to walk for hours. A brisk 10-minute daily walk has health benefits and counts toward the 150 minutes of weekly activity recommended for adults.

What is "brisk"? About 3 miles an hour, faster than a stroll. The talk test works well: you can still talk but cannot sing the words of a song. The free NHS Active 10 app can also tell you whether you are walking fast enough.

Getting started: comfortable, supportive shoes that do not cause blisters are enough. For longer walks, carry water, a snack, a spare layer, sunscreen and a hat.

If you are not very active, build up distance gradually. If joints are a problem, water-based exercise classes can ease the load while still strengthening muscles. If you cannot leave the house, indoor exercise videos are an option.

Staying motivated is mostly about habit: walk part of the way to work, use stairs, leave the car behind for short trips, or walk with a friend or the family. Setting small, specific goals and noticing progress keeps momentum.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: step counts from a phone or watch give a gentle picture of the week. A run of low-step days can prompt a friendly offer, such as a ten-minute walk after lunch, rather than a verdict. People with pain, breathlessness, or a health condition should check with a clinician about suitable activity.

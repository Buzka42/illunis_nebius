# Exercise basics: habit, progression and variety

MedlinePlus frames fitness as a few simple ideas.

- **Make it a habit.** The body adapts to what it does most often, so regular practice brings improvement.
- **Build up slowly.** Gain strength, speed or flexibility without pushing too hard at once.
- **Challenge yourself a little.** Slightly heavier weights, a few more minutes on a walk, or a slightly faster pace.
- **Mix it up.** Variety keeps things interesting and helps prevent injury and burnout.
- **Show up regularly.** Even short sessions keep progress going.

Main types of exercise:

- **Aerobic** (walking, running, swimming) raises breathing and heart rate and supports heart, lungs and circulation.
- **Strength or resistance** training, using weights, bands or body weight, builds muscle.
- **Balance** work, such as tai chi or standing on one leg, helps stability.
- **Flexibility** work, such as stretching, helps keep joints moving.

Fitting it in: you can break exercise into chunks, and small amounts help. You do not need fancy equipment. Options include home (mat, bands, a bike or a video), a gym with staff guidance, or outdoors (walking, cycling, parks). The page suggests working the core, meaning abdomen, back and hips, since it supports balance and may lower the risk of back injury. People who have not exercised in a while, or who have trouble moving or balancing, should check with a health provider before starting.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: when a user is restarting, suggest a very small version of the habit and a gentle step up the following week. Treat missed days as normal. Training recovery and injury questions can be passed to the training or musculoskeletal expert.

# Weekly activity targets for adults

UK guidance for adults aged 19 to 64 has four parts:

- Do some physical activity every day. Even once or twice a week lowers the risk of heart disease and stroke compared with none.
- Aim for at least **150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a week**, or **75 minutes of vigorous activity**, or a mix of both. Short, very vigorous bursts can also count toward the total.
- Spread it over 4 to 5 days, or every day, rather than saving it for one day.
- Do **strengthening activities for all the major muscle groups** (legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders, arms) on at least 2 days a week.
- Sit or lie down less, and break up long still periods with a little movement.

How to judge intensity. Moderate means your heart rate and breathing rise and you feel warmer, but you can still talk and not sing. Examples are brisk walking, cycling, dancing, doubles tennis and hiking. Vigorous means you breathe hard and fast and can only say a few words at a time, such as running, swimming, fast cycling, football or skipping. Roughly, 75 minutes of vigorous activity gives similar benefit to 150 minutes of moderate activity.

The guidelines also suit disabled adults and people who are pregnant or recently gave birth, with activity choices matched to prior fitness. The page suggests speaking to a GP first if you have not exercised in a long time or have a medical condition.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: describe the week as a total (for example, "about 90 minutes of brisk walking so far") against 150 minutes rather than calling a day good or bad. If wearable steps drop for a few days, offer gentle ways to rebuild. Anyone with chest pain, dizziness or unusual breathlessness during activity should stop and speak to a clinician.

# Reading food labels

Pre-packed food in the UK carries a nutrition label on the back or side. It states energy (kJ and kcal), fat, saturated fat, carbohydrate, sugars, protein and salt, sometimes with fibre. Values are given per 100 g or 100 ml, and often per portion. Because the per-100 g column is the same for every product, it is the fair way to compare two foods.

The page gives rule-of-thumb levels per 100 g for "high" and "low":

- Total fat: high above 17.5 g, low at 3 g or less.
- Saturated fat: high above 5 g, low at 1.5 g or less.
- Total sugars: high above 22.5 g, low at 5 g or less.
- Salt: high above 1.5 g, low at 0.3 g or less.

Different thresholds apply to drinks. Many supermarkets also add colour-coded labels on the front, where red means high, amber medium and green low. Foods high in fat, salt and sugar are best eaten less often and in small amounts.

Useful habits: compare similar products by the per-100 g column, check the portion size the label is using (it may be smaller than what you really eat), and check the sugars line on drinks and breakfast cereals. Labels are a tool for choice, not a score to chase for every meal.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: when a user asks "is this a good choice?", answer by pointing to which label lines are high or low and what the alternatives are. Avoid calorie targets or restriction advice, and suggest a clinician or dietitian for anyone who is worried about their eating or weight.

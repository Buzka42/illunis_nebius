# Drinks and staying hydrated

Water is a cheap, healthy way to stay hydrated, but other drinks count too, and we also get fluid from food. Not drinking enough can lead to dehydration.

A simple everyday check from the guidance: aim to drink enough that your urine is a clear, pale yellow. The Eatwell Guide suggests about 6 to 8 cups or glasses of fluid a day as a guide, not a rule. Needs are higher in hot weather, during long periods of physical activity, when ill, and during pregnancy or breastfeeding.

What counts: water, lower-fat milk and lower-sugar drinks, including tea and coffee.

Tips from the page:

- Drink regularly through the day instead of all at once.
- Swap sugary drinks for sugar-free or no-added-sugar ones, and dilute squash well.
- Keep fruit juice and smoothies to one small glass (150 ml) a day, ideally with a meal, because they are high in sugar and can harm teeth.
- Read drink labels and prefer those marked green or amber.
- Take caffeine in moderation. Sensitivity varies from person to person.
- After sweating or when unwell, water is the best way to replace lost fluid.
- If plain water is dull, sparkling water or a no-added-sugar flavouring may help.

For children, water and milk are the best drinks, and sugary drinks are best avoided.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: pairing a drink with an existing habit (a glass with each meal, a bottle at the desk) is more reliable than a number target. Colour of urine is a gentle self-check; strongly dark urine, or signs of dehydration that do not improve with fluids, is a reason to ask a clinician.

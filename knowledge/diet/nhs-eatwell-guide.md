# The Eatwell Guide: balance across food groups

The Eatwell Guide is the UK's picture of what a balanced diet looks like. The key idea is proportion, and it does not have to be met at every meal. Getting the balance roughly right over a day, or even a week, is the aim.

The guide groups food like this:

- **Fruit and vegetables** make up a little over a third of what we eat. At least five portions a day, in a variety. Fresh, frozen, tinned, dried and juiced all count, but juice and smoothies together are best kept to about 150 ml a day.
- **Starchy foods** such as potatoes, bread, rice and pasta make up another third or so. Higher-fibre and wholegrain versions, or potatoes with the skin on, add fibre.
- **Protein foods**: beans, pulses, fish, eggs and meat. Pulses are a low-fat, high-fibre option. The guide suggests less red and processed meat, and at least two portions of fish a week, one of them oily.
- **Dairy and alternatives**: milk, yoghurt, cheese, or fortified soya options. They supply calcium for bones. Lower-fat and lower-sugar choices are suggested.
- **Oils and spreads**: choose unsaturated types, in small amounts, because every fat is energy-dense.
- **Foods and drinks high in fat, salt and sugar**, such as cakes, biscuits, chocolate and sugary drinks, are not needed and are best eaten less often and in small amounts.

Fluids matter too, so the guide sits alongside advice to drink regularly through the day.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: describe what a day of eating looks like against these proportions without judging single meals, and offer one small swap at a time (for example, a wholegrain choice or an extra vegetable portion). The guide describes the general population and is not a personal plan.

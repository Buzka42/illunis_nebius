# Insomnia: what it is and when to get help

Insomnia means regularly having problems sleeping. The NHS describes it as usually improving with changes to sleeping habits. Signs include difficulty falling asleep, waking several times, lying awake, waking early and not getting back to sleep, feeling tired after waking, daytime irritability and trouble concentrating. Under three months is short-term insomnia; three months or more is long-term.

Sleep needs vary. On average adults need 7 to 9 hours, children 9 to 13, and babies and toddlers 12 to 17. Constant daytime tiredness suggests too little sleep.

Common contributors: stress, anxiety or depression; noise; a room that is too hot or cold; an uncomfortable bed; alcohol, caffeine or nicotine; recreational drugs; jet lag; and shift work. Some health conditions and medicines can also disrupt sleep, as can long-term pain, snoring or interrupted breathing, and restless legs.

Self-help from the page includes: go to bed only when sleepy; keep the same wake time every day; relax for at least an hour before bed; keep the bedroom dark and quiet; exercise regularly during the day but not within about four hours of bed; avoid caffeine and alcohol for around six hours before bed; do not eat a big meal late; avoid screens right before bed; avoid daytime naps; do not sleep in after a bad night; and do not drive when sleepy.

When to see a clinician: if changing habits has not helped, if trouble sleeping has lasted months, or if it makes daily life hard to cope with. A GP looks for causes and may offer cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), face to face or online. A sleep clinic may be suggested if another sleep disorder is suspected. The page says sleeping pills are now rarely prescribed because of side effects and dependence. Anything you take for sleep, including pharmacy products, should be checked with a doctor or pharmacist first.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: mention patterns the user has noticed and suggest the clinician path for persistent problems. Do not suggest products or doses.

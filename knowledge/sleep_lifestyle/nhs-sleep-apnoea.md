# Sleep apnoea: signs worth checking

Sleep apnoea is when breathing stops and starts during sleep. The most common kind is obstructive sleep apnoea, where the throat narrows or closes. The NHS says it needs treating because it can lead to more serious problems.

Signs during sleep, often noticed by a partner: breathing that stops and starts, gasping, snorting or choking noises, waking a lot, and loud snoring. Signs by day: feeling very tired, difficulty concentrating, mood swings, and headache on waking. It can be hard to tell on your own, so it helps to ask someone to watch for symptoms.

When to see a clinician: the NHS advises seeing a GP if you have the main symptoms, such as breathing that stops and starts, gasping or choking at night, or being always very tired in the daytime. If someone else has seen the symptoms, bring them along. Untreated sleep apnoea can be serious.

What happens next, in general terms: a GP may refer you to a specialist sleep clinic. Tests use devices worn overnight, often at home, that record breathing and heartbeat. A severity score based on how often breathing pauses (the AHI score) helps decide what care is needed. Lifestyle factors such as weight, alcohol and sleeping position can matter, and the clinician will advise.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: if a user reports persistent daytime exhaustion despite enough hours in bed, or a partner mentions pauses in breathing, suggest raising it with a GP rather than adjusting habits alone. Do not try to assess severity or suggest devices. Wearable sleep data cannot diagnose sleep apnoea.

# What sleep does and how much we need

MedlinePlus explains that sleep is an active process. The brain cycles through non-REM sleep (three stages, light to deep) and REM sleep, where most dreaming occurs. Breathing, heart rate, body temperature and brain waves change through the night, and all the stages matter.

Sleep helps you feel rested and energetic, supports learning and memory, gives the heart and blood vessels a rest, releases growth hormone that helps repair muscle and tissue, supports hormones involved in puberty and fertility, helps keep hunger and fullness hormones in balance, and strengthens the immune system.

General recommended amounts per day: babies (4 to 12 months) 12 to 16 hours including naps; children 1 to 2 years 11 to 14; 3 to 5 years 10 to 13; 6 to 12 years 9 to 12; teens 8 to 10; adults 7 to 9 hours. Needs depend on age, lifestyle and health. During puberty the body clock shifts later, which is one reason many teens struggle with early school starts.

Because the page covers "sleep hygiene", the common habit advice applies: a consistent schedule, a calm, dark, cool bedroom, less caffeine and alcohol near bedtime, daytime activity, and winding down without screens.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: compare the user's average sleep to the 7 to 9 hour adult range gently, noting that individual needs vary, and that sleep quality matters as well as length. Consumer wearables estimate sleep stages roughly and should not be treated as precise. Persistent tiredness despite adequate time in bed is worth discussing with a clinician.

# Atrial fibrillation: an irregular heartbeat

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is a heart rhythm problem in which the heartbeat is not steady. The NHS says to see a GP if you think you may have it.

Typical features: an irregular pulse; sudden pounding, racing, fluttering or skipping beats (palpitations) lasting seconds to minutes; a heartbeat faster than normal; unusual tiredness; finding exercise harder; chest tightness; breathlessness; light-headedness or feeling faint. Sometimes there are no symptoms and AF is found at a routine check or while testing for something else. It can come and go, persist, or become permanent.

Who is more likely to have it: people 55 or older, men, people living with overweight or obesity, smokers, and endurance athletes; also people with high blood pressure, heart conditions, kidney disease, an overactive thyroid, diabetes or sleep apnoea, and some medicines. Anyone can get it.

General self-help from the page: avoid triggers such as caffeine, spicy food, stress, dehydration and lack of sleep; keep a healthy weight; eat a balanced diet; do regular moderate activity; do not smoke; limit alcohol; and avoid exercising too hard, which can trigger AF.

Why it matters: AF raises the risk of blood clots, stroke and heart failure, so it needs medical assessment. A GP may refer you to a heart specialist for tests, such as an ECG.

When to get help: see a GP if you think you have symptoms or palpitations keep happening or worsen. Call emergency services immediately if you have a fast or irregular heartbeat with chest pain, breathlessness, sweating, feeling sick, fainting, severe headache, weakness or numbness on one side, blurred vision, or confusion.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: a wearable's irregular-rhythm alert is a reason to speak to a clinician, not something to interpret. Not medical advice.

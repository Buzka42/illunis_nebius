# Stroke: the FAST signs and why time matters

A stroke is when blood stops flowing to part of the brain. It can affect speech and movement and take a long time to recover from. The NHS says it needs urgent hospital help because it can be life-threatening.

The main signs can happen suddenly and are remembered with **FAST**:

- **Face**: one side may droop, and smiling may be hard.
- **Arms**: you may not be able to lift both arms and keep them there, because of weakness or numbness in one.
- **Speech**: words may be slurred or confused.
- **Time**: call emergency services straight away.

Other signs: weakness or numbness down one side of the body; blurred vision or loss of sight in one or both eyes; difficulty speaking or finding words; confusion and memory loss; dizziness or falling; severe headache; and feeling or being sick.

Symptoms can sometimes stop after a short time, so a person may think they are fine. The NHS says to get medical help straight away even then. A stroke is more likely at older ages but can happen at any age.

Call emergency services now if you think you or someone else is having or has had a stroke, or if there were signs within the last 24 hours even if they have gone. Do not drive yourself to hospital; the call handler will advise what to do.

Reducing long-term risk links to blood pressure, cholesterol, smoking, alcohol, activity and diet, covered in other notes. Support organisations such as the Stroke Association provide information.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: if a user describes any of these signs, even briefly resolved ones, say clearly to call emergency services now. Do not use wearable data to rule a stroke in or out. Not medical advice.

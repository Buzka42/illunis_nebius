# Cardiovascular disease: the group of conditions

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is a name for a group of conditions that affect the heart and blood vessels. The NHS notes it can often be prevented through a healthy lifestyle, or with medicines for those who need them. It is very common and one of the main causes of death in the UK.

Main types listed: coronary heart disease (arteries blocked by fatty build-up, making it harder for blood to reach the heart), peripheral arterial disease (reduced blood flow to the legs and feet), aortic disease (conditions of the body's largest blood vessel) and stroke (sudden loss of blood flow to part of the brain). The page cautions against trying to diagnose yourself.

Symptoms depend on the condition, and some people have none. For some, the first sign is chest pain (angina), a heart attack or a stroke.

Who is at higher risk: people who smoke, drink heavily, are overweight or inactive; those over 50; men more than women; people of South Asian, Black African or African Caribbean background; people with relatives with CVD; and people with diabetes, high blood pressure or high cholesterol.

Reducing risk: eat a balanced diet with less fatty food, exercise regularly, keep a healthy weight, cut down on alcohol, and stop smoking. The NHS Health Check, a free check for people aged 40 to 74, helps estimate risk. Some people at higher risk are advised to take cholesterol-lowering medicine by a clinician.

When to see a clinician: see a GP if you think you are at higher risk. Treat chest pain, stroke signs or other emergency symptoms as emergencies (see the heart attack and stroke notes).

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: describe heart-healthy habits and the value of routine checks without implying anyone has or lacks a condition. Not medical advice.

# High cholesterol: a silent risk factor

Cholesterol is a fatty substance in the blood. Having too much of it can build up in blood vessels, which makes heart problems and stroke more likely. The NHS says high cholesterol does not usually cause symptoms, and the only way to find out is a blood test.

Who is more likely to have it: people over 50, men, people who have been through the menopause, people of South Asian or sub-Saharan African origin, and those with a family history. Lifestyle contributors include eating fatty food, not being active enough, being overweight, smoking and drinking alcohol. Some forms run in families and begin young (familial hypercholesterolaemia), and the NHS points to HEART UK for information on that.

What the page says helps: eating healthily and getting more exercise can lower cholesterol, and some people also need medicine, which a clinician decides on.

Related dietary context from the NHS fat guidance: eating less saturated fat and replacing some with unsaturated fat, such as oils from plants and oily fish, supports healthier cholesterol. More fibre, from oats, pulses and vegetables, is part of a heart-friendly pattern.

When to see a clinician: because there are no warning signs, ask about a cholesterol test if you have risk factors, a family history of early heart disease or high cholesterol, or if you are due an NHS Health Check (40 to 74). Do not try to interpret numbers alone.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: link food and activity choices to heart health in general terms, without giving targets or stating that anyone has high cholesterol. Questions about test results or medicines are for a clinician. Not medical advice.

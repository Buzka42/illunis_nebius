# Heart attack: recognise it and act fast

A heart attack happens when blood flow to the heart is blocked. The NHS stresses that it can be life-threatening and needs emergency treatment in hospital.

Symptoms may include: chest pain that feels crushing or squeezing and may spread to the arm, neck or jaw; shortness of breath; feeling or being sick; a feeling like indigestion, with burning, fullness or bloating; sweating; and pale, blue or grey skin (on brown or black skin this can be easier to see on the palms).

Call emergency services immediately if: you have chest pain that feels tight or squeezing, chest pain that spreads to the arms, neck or jaw, severe difficulty breathing, pale, blue or grey lips or skin, or someone has passed out and is not responding normally. Do not drive yourself to hospital; the call handler will advise. Call NHS 111 if you are suddenly more breathless than usual, or have chest pain with sickness, or a racing, slow or fluttering heartbeat.

While waiting for help, as the NHS advises: sit and rest in a comfortable, supported position, keep checking that the person is responding and breathing, and start CPR if they stop responding and stop breathing. Use only the instructions of the emergency call handler for any medicine. A person with diagnosed angina may have their own prescribed spray.

In hospital, an ECG and blood tests confirm a heart attack, and further tests such as an echocardiogram or angiogram show damage and where blood flow is blocked.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: if a user describes these symptoms, the assistant should say plainly to call emergency services now, and should not discuss lifestyle or wait for more information. Not medical advice.

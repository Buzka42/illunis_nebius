# Angina: chest tightness that needs checking

Angina is a feeling of pain or tightness, usually in the chest. The NHS says it can be a sign of a heart problem and advises seeing a GP as soon as possible if you think you may have it.

What it can feel like: sudden pain in the chest, neck, shoulders, jaw or arms, such as tightness, squeezing, pressure or a dull ache, sometimes with sickness, breathlessness, dizziness or sweating. Attacks are often brought on by exercise, stress, emotion or cold, though sometimes there is no clear trigger. They typically last under ten minutes and ease with rest or the person's prescribed medicine.

Why it happens: the heart is not getting enough blood, most often because arteries supplying it are narrowed by fatty deposits. Risk is higher with smoking, heavy drinking, overweight, high blood pressure or cholesterol, earlier heart problems, relatives with heart problems, and conditions such as diabetes, arthritis or kidney disease.

General living advice: avoid known triggers such as stress, cold and large meals as far as possible, keep prescribed medicine with you, stop smoking, keep a healthy weight, eat a balanced diet, and be as active as symptoms allow. Talk to a doctor before starting a new activity if you are worried about exercise.

When to get help: contact NHS 111 for chest pain that comes and goes, or if diagnosed angina feels worse, happens more often, lasts longer or occurs at rest. Call emergency services immediately if chest pain does not stop with rest and the person's prescribed medicine, or for sudden chest pain that does not go away, spreads to the arm, neck, jaw or back, or comes with sweating, sickness, light-headedness or breathlessness. Do not drive yourself to hospital.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: never interpret chest symptoms. State the urgent route plainly and stay out of treatment details. Not medical advice.

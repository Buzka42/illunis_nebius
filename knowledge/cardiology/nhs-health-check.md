# The NHS Health Check: a routine look at heart health

The NHS Health Check is a free check-up of cardiovascular health. It helps show whether you are at higher risk of heart disease, diabetes, kidney disease or stroke, and gives you a chance to discuss how to reduce that risk. For people over 65 it also covers symptoms of dementia to look out for, since some types share risk factors.

Who it is for: adults aged 40 to 74 without existing conditions such as heart disease, chronic kidney disease, diabetes, high blood pressure, atrial fibrillation, stroke or transient ischaemic attack, heart failure, peripheral arterial disease, inherited high cholesterol, or a current prescription for cholesterol-lowering medicine. Those with such conditions get regular reviews through their care team. Eligible people should be invited every five years by their GP or local council, and can ask if they have not been invited.

What happens: a pharmacist, nurse or healthcare assistant usually carries it out in about 20 to 30 minutes. It includes height, weight and waist measurements, a blood pressure test, a cholesterol test and possibly a blood sugar test. You will be asked about family history, smoking, alcohol and physical activity. Age, sex and ethnicity are recorded. Results are usually discussed during the appointment, with advice on lifestyle changes.

Why it is useful: many risk factors, including blood pressure and cholesterol, have no symptoms, so checking is the only way to know.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: if a user is 40 or older and has never had a check, suggest asking their GP surgery or local pharmacy. Frame it as routine prevention, not a sign something is wrong. Rules differ by country and region. Not medical advice.

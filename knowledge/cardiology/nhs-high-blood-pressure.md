# High blood pressure: why checking matters

High blood pressure (hypertension) is very common, especially in older adults, and usually causes no symptoms, so many people do not know they have it. The only way to find out is to have it measured. Rarely it can cause headaches, blurred vision or chest pain.

Things that raise the chance: older age, close relatives with it, a Black African, Black Caribbean or South Asian background, a diet high in salt, being overweight, smoking, heavy drinking and long-term stress.

Why it matters: the extra strain on blood vessels, heart and organs such as the brain, kidneys and eyes raises the risk of heart disease, heart attack, stroke, heart failure, kidney disease and vascular dementia. Lowering blood pressure even a little reduces those risks.

A check uses an inflatable cuff on the upper arm. If a reading is high, a clinician may ask for home readings over several days or a portable monitor worn for 24 hours to confirm. Readings are interpreted by a clinician against thresholds that differ between clinic and home measurement.

General lifestyle habits the NHS lists: a balanced diet, regular activity (at least 150 minutes a week), losing weight if overweight, less salt, less alcohol, moderate caffeine and not smoking. Some people also need medicines, which a clinician selects.

When to seek help: get checked at a pharmacy or GP surgery if you think you might be at risk, or if you are 40 or over and have not had a check in five years. Contact NHS 111 if you often have headaches or blurred vision, have chest pain that comes and goes, or other worrying symptoms. Call emergency services immediately for sudden chest pain or pressure that does not go away, pain spreading to the arm, neck, jaw or back, or chest pain with sweating, sickness, light-headedness or breathlessness.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: it can describe habits that support heart health, but must not interpret readings or say whether someone has hypertension. Not medical advice.

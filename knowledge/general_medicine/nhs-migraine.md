# Migraine

Migraine is a condition causing symptoms such as a severe headache, nausea and sensitivity to light. The NHS says treatment can help manage symptoms.

Main symptoms of an attack: a throbbing headache, often on one side, that can be severe and worse with movement; feeling or being sick; and sensitivity to light and sound. Early (warning-phase) symptoms can include feeling very tired and yawning, craving certain foods or thirst, mood changes, a stiff neck and peeing more. Some people have aura, such as zigzag lines or flashing lights, numbness or pins and needles, dizziness or difficulty speaking; aura should not last longer than an hour. Attacks usually last between 4 hours and 3 days, and early symptoms can begin up to two days before the head pain. Some people have attacks several times a week, others rarely. Menstrual migraines, around a period, are often longer and more severe.

See a GP if attacks are severe, getting worse or lasting longer than usual; you have attacks more than once a week; you find it hard to control them; or you regularly get migraine before or during your period.

Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 if an attack has lasted longer than 72 hours; aura symptoms last longer than an hour at a time; or you are pregnant or have just had a baby.

Call emergency services for symptoms such as a sudden, severe headache or signs of stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, speech problems), as listed on the NHS page and in the headaches note.

Everyday links: sleep, regular meals, hydration, caffeine, alcohol and stress are commonly named triggers, and noting personal patterns in a diary can help a clinician. Individual triggers vary.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: a user who tracks sleep, energy and mood may see patterns before attacks; reflect them as observations. Do not recommend medicines. Not medical advice.

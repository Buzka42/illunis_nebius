# Headaches: common and usually harmless, with some red flags

Most headaches go away on their own and are not a sign of something more serious. They can last 30 minutes, several hours, or sometimes days.

Easing them, from the NHS: drink plenty of water; rest if you also have a cold or flu; try to relax, since stress can make headaches worse; and ask a pharmacist about pain relief. Avoid skipping meals, sleeping much more than usual (it can worsen headaches), straining your eyes for long periods such as looking at a screen, and alcohol. Stay home and avoid others if you also have a high temperature or feel unwell.

See a GP if you have tried treating a headache and it is not getting better or is getting worse, or if you get headaches regularly.

Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 if you or a child has a headache with vision or eye problems; a headache triggered or made worse by coughing, sneezing, bending down or exercise; a headache with vomiting; for a child, a headache that is getting worse or wakes them at night; headache with jaw pain when eating or a sore, tender scalp; or any other symptom that worries you.

Call emergency services or go to A&E if a headache comes with a seizure; numbness or weakness in the body or face; sudden, extremely painful onset; a head injury within the last three months; difficulty speaking, balancing, walking or remembering; drowsiness or confusion; loss of vision; a rash that does not fade when a glass is rolled over it; or a very high temperature with a stiff neck or sensitivity to bright lights.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: for a common tension-type headache after a stressful, poorly slept day, suggest water, a break from screens and a short walk. Always flag the red-flag list when a user describes a sudden, severe or unusual headache. Not medical advice.

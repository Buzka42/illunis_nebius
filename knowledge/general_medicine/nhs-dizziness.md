# Dizziness

Feeling dizzy, lightheaded or off-balance now and then is common and not usually serious, says the NHS. Dizziness covers feeling off-balance, giddy, lightheaded or faint, and vertigo, the sense that you or the room is spinning.

Self-care while it lasts: lie down until it passes and then get up slowly; move slowly and carefully; rest; drink plenty of fluids, especially water; and avoid coffee, cigarettes, alcohol and drugs. Avoid bending down suddenly and getting up suddenly after sitting or lying. Do not do anything that could be dangerous while dizzy, such as driving, climbing a ladder or using heavy machinery.

See a GP if you are worried about dizziness or vertigo; if it will not go away or keeps coming back; if you are finding it harder to hear or speak; if there is ringing or other sounds in the ears (tinnitus); if you have double or blurred vision or other eyesight changes; if your face, arms or legs feel numb or weak; or if you also have a changed pulse, fainting or collapsing, headaches or sickness.

Causes: many, including being ill with something else, standing up quickly, dehydration, and inner-ear problems. The page cautions against self-diagnosing and advises seeing a GP if worried.

Important cross-reference: sudden dizziness with face drooping, arm weakness, speech problems, severe headache or sudden vision loss can be a sign of stroke, and needs emergency help straight away (see the stroke note). Dizziness with chest pain, breathlessness or fainting also needs urgent attention.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: when a user reports dizziness after standing, suggest sitting down, fluids and moving slowly, and mention the warning signs. A watch's heart-rate numbers should not be used to judge the cause. Not medical advice.

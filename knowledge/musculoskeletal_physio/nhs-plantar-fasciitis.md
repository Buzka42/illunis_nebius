# Plantar fasciitis: heel and arch pain

Plantar fasciitis causes pain on the bottom of the foot, around the heel and arch. The NHS says you can usually ease it yourself, but to see a GP if it does not improve within two weeks.

Clues that it may be plantar fasciitis: the pain is much worse with the first steps after sleeping or resting; it eases as you start moving but worsens after long periods of standing, walking or running; and it hurts to stretch the sole of the foot, for example lifting your toes off the floor or climbing stairs.

Self-care from the page: rest and raise the foot when you can; apply an ice pack or wrapped bag of frozen peas for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours; wear shoes with cushioned heels and good arch support; use insoles or heel pads; do gentle regular stretches for the sole and heel; choose low-pressure exercise such as swimming; ask a pharmacist about pain relief; and lose weight if overweight. Avoid long periods of walking or standing, high heels, tight pointy shoes, flip-flops, backless slippers and walking barefoot on hard surfaces.

See a GP if the pain is severe or stops normal activities, is getting worse or keeps coming back, has not improved after two weeks of self-care, comes with tingling or loss of feeling in the foot, or if you have diabetes and foot pain, since foot problems can be more serious with diabetes. A foot specialist may offer further treatment.

Relevance to the training expert: runners and people on their feet for work are commonly affected. Reducing impact, changing footwear and building mileage gradually are general ideas, but persistent pain deserves proper assessment.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: suggest footwear and stretching as options and the two-week rule for seeing a clinician. Not medical advice.

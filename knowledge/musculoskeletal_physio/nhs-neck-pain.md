# Neck pain: keep moving and look after posture

Most neck pain lasts only a few weeks. The NHS says there are things you can do at home, and to see a GP if it does not go away.

Common causes: the neck getting stuck in an awkward position during sleep, poor posture such as long periods at a desk, a pinched nerve, and injuries such as whiplash after a traffic accident or fall.

Self-care ideas: a low, firm pillow; heat or cold packs (a wrapped bag of frozen peas for a short time, or a wrapped hot-water bottle, for example); gentle neck flexibility exercises; and pain relief advised by a pharmacist. The page recommends not wearing a neck collar, because it is better to keep the neck moving unless a doctor says otherwise. Avoid anything unsafe when you cannot turn your head easily, such as driving or cycling.

Prevention: when sleeping, keep the head level with the rest of the body; use a firm mattress; sit upright with shoulders gently back; avoid holding the neck in one position for long periods at a desk; do not sleep on your front; and avoid twisting the neck in bed.

When to see a clinician: see a GP if neck pain or stiffness does not go away after a few weeks, if usual pain relief has not worked, if you are worried, or if you have other symptoms such as pins and needles or a cold arm, which could be something more serious. In many areas you can refer yourself for physiotherapy through NHS community musculoskeletal services.

Related to the training expert: short mobility breaks can offset long periods of sitting. An NHS flexibility routine includes gentle neck rotations and stretches.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: suggest posture breaks and gentle movement, and flag nerve-type symptoms such as numbness or arm weakness for a clinician. After a head or neck injury or with sudden severe pain, seek urgent help. Not medical advice.

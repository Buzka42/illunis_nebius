# Tendonitis: swollen tendons after overuse or injury

Tendonitis is when a tendon, the tissue connecting muscle to bone, swells and becomes inflamed after an injury. It can cause joint pain and stiffness. The NHS says mild cases can be treated at home and should feel better within two to three weeks. Tendons in knees, elbows and shoulders are common sites.

Signs: pain in a tendon that worsens when you move; difficulty moving the joint; a grating or crackling feeling on movement; and swelling, sometimes with heat or redness.

Typical causes: sudden sharp movements or repetitive exercise such as running, jumping or throwing.

Home care for the first two to three days: **rest** by avoiding moving the tendon, **ice** wrapped in a cloth for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours, and **support** with a snug (not tight) elastic bandage or soft brace, removed before bed. When you can move the area without pain stopping you, keep it moving so the joint does not stiffen. Avoid heavy lifting, strong gripping or twisting that makes symptoms worse, and avoid sport until the tendon has recovered. A pharmacist can recommend pain relief.

Prevention ideas given by the NHS include warming up properly, building up exercise gradually, and not repeating one movement for long periods.

If more help is needed, a GP may suggest physiotherapy. For severe or long-lasting cases other options include injections for short-term relief or an orthopaedic specialist's opinion, with surgery in some cases. A ruptured tendon needs urgent assessment.

See a GP if you have injured a joint and symptoms do not improve within a few weeks. Get help from NHS 111 if you are in a lot of pain or think you have ruptured a tendon.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: the training expert can discuss gradual load increases and rest days in general terms. A sudden pop followed by weakness or inability to use the limb needs prompt medical assessment. Not medical advice.

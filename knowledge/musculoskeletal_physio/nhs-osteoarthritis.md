# Osteoarthritis: painful, stiff joints

Osteoarthritis is the most common type of arthritis in the UK. The NHS describes it as a condition that makes joints painful and stiff.

Main symptoms: joint pain, stiffness and problems moving the joint, sometimes with swelling, tenderness, and a grating or crackling sound. Severity varies widely. For some people symptoms are mild and come and go, and for others they are continuous and make daily tasks hard. Any joint can be affected, but knees, hips and small joints of the hands are most often involved. Redness, where present, can be harder to see on brown and black skin.

What happens: normally the body repairs everyday joint wear without symptoms. In osteoarthritis the protective cartilage on the ends of bones breaks down, which leads to pain, swelling and difficulty moving, and bony growths may form. The exact cause is unknown. Factors thought to raise risk include joint injury or overuse, older age, being overweight, a family history and certain other conditions.

The NHS page describes lifestyle measures such as exercise, good posture and weight management, with a structured exercise plan from a physiotherapist and pain relief for more severe symptoms, and surgery to repair, strengthen or replace a joint in a small number of cases. For prevention it suggests at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week plus strength work on two or more days, regular movement breaks if you sit at a desk, and a healthy weight, since extra weight adds strain on joints. The training and musculoskeletal experts can discuss low-impact movement in general terms such as walking, swimming and cycling, plus strengthening for muscles around the joint.

When to see a clinician: see a GP if you have persistent symptoms, so the diagnosis can be confirmed and any needed treatment arranged.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: encourage gentle regular movement and pacing activities, and avoid prescribing exercises for painful joints. Not medical advice.

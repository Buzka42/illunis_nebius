# Sprains and strains

Sprains and strains are common injuries to ligaments and muscles. The NHS says most can be treated at home. A **sprain** is a torn or twisted ligament (tissue connecting joints), common in wrists, ankles, thumbs and knees. A **strain** is an overstretched or torn muscle (a pulled muscle), common in feet, legs and back.

Signs: pain, tenderness or weakness, usually around the ankle, foot, wrist, thumb, knee, leg or back; swelling or bruising; trouble putting weight on the injury or using it normally; and muscle spasms or cramping.

For the first two to three days the NHS suggests the **PRICE** steps:

- **Protection**: use a support or supportive shoes.
- **Rest**: stop exercise and try not to put weight on it.
- **Ice**: a cold pack wrapped in a cloth for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours.
- **Compression**: a bandage for support in the daytime.
- **Elevation**: keep it raised as much as possible.

Avoid heat, alcohol and massage for the first couple of days to limit swelling. Once you can move without pain stopping you, gently keep the joint or muscle moving so it does not stiffen. A pharmacist can recommend pain relief. Most sprains and strains feel better after about two weeks, but the page advises avoiding strenuous exercise such as running for up to eight weeks to reduce the risk of further damage. Physiotherapy may help if recovery is slow.

Get help from NHS 111 if the injury is very painful or getting worse, there is a lot of swelling or bruising, you cannot put weight on it or walk more than a few steps, it is very stiff, it is not improving, or you have a high temperature or shivers. See a GP if it is getting worse or not improving with self-care.

Call emergency services or go to A&E if you heard a crack, the body part changed shape or points at an odd angle, it is numb or tingling, or the skin is blue, grey or cold. You may have a broken bone.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: tie return to activity to pain and function, not to a calendar. Not medical advice.

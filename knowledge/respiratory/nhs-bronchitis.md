# Bronchitis: inflamed airways

Bronchitis is inflammation of the airways in the lungs, usually caused by an infection. The NHS says it often gets better without treatment in around three weeks. Long-term inflammation of the airways, chronic bronchitis, is part of COPD.

Symptoms resemble a cold or flu: a cough that may bring up mucus, chest pain when coughing, shortness of breath, a sore throat, a runny nose and a high temperature.

Looking after yourself: get plenty of rest, drink plenty of fluids, stay home and avoid contact with others if you have a high temperature or feel unwell, and ask a pharmacist about pain relief. Infection spreads through coughs and sneezes, so good hygiene helps. Avoiding smoke is also advisable.

Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 if you have had a cough for more than three weeks that is not improving; cough up blood or blood-stained mucus; have chest pain that comes and goes or occurs with breathing or coughing; are over 65 or pregnant; have a long-term condition such as diabetes or a heart, lung or kidney condition; have a weakened immune system; feel too unwell for daily activities; or feel short of breath. For a child aged 4 or under, call 111 for advice.

Call emergency services if you are struggling to breathe (choking, gasping, unable to speak); your lips or skin turn very pale, blue or grey; you suddenly feel confused; or you cannot wake a baby or they feel floppy. Do not drive yourself.

Treatment: it usually clears without treatment in about three weeks, so see a GP if symptoms last longer than that.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: suggest rest and fluids for a mild chest cold and be alert to the warning signs, particularly for older people and those with long-term conditions. Not medical advice.

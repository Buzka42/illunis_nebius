# Shortness of breath: when it is urgent

Shortness of breath might be nothing to worry about, but sometimes it is serious and needs medical help. The NHS lists clear emergency signs.

Call emergency services or go to A&E immediately if you have severe difficulty breathing (gasping, choking, unable to get words out); your chest feels tight or heavy; you have pain spreading to the arms, back, neck or jaw; your lips or skin turn very pale, blue or grey (on brown or black skin, check the palms); or you suddenly feel confused. For a child, urgent signs include difficulty breathing with grunting or sucking the stomach in under the ribs, pale, blue or grey skin or lips, sudden confusion or agitation, or being limp, floppy or unresponsive. Do not drive yourself.

Contact NHS 111 if you are more breathless than usual, feel or are sick, cough up blood, have pain or swelling in one leg, or have heart palpitations (a racing, slow, skipping or fluttering heartbeat).

See a GP if breathlessness gets worse during normal activities or when lying down, if you are breathless with swollen ankles, or if you have been coughing for three weeks or more. The page says you are not wasting anyone's time by getting it checked.

Common causes include asthma, a chest infection, being overweight, and other lung and heart conditions, along with anxiety and panic attacks. Because the causes range from minor to serious, the symptom itself should not be assessed by an app.

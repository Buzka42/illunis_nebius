# Cough: usually short-lived

A cough usually clears up on its own within three to four weeks, and the NHS says there is normally no need to see a GP. For self-care: rest, drink plenty of fluids, and stay at home and avoid others if you have a high temperature or do not feel well enough for normal activities.

Home remedies mentioned: a warm drink of lemon and honey (not for babies under one, and not hot for small children), and some herbal products, though the page notes limited evidence that these help. Some medicines and herbal products are not safe for everyone, for example in pregnancy, so check the leaflet or ask a pharmacist. A pharmacist can talk through cough syrups and sweets, which may help you cough less but will not stop a cough. The page says decongestants and codeine-containing cough medicines will not stop a cough, and some cough medicines should not be given to children under 12.

See a GP if the cough has lasted more than three weeks (persistent cough), you are losing weight for no reason, or you have a weakened immune system, for example during chemotherapy or with diabetes.

Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 if your cough is very bad or getting worse quickly, such as a hacking cough you cannot stop; you feel very unwell; you have chest pain; the side of your neck is swollen and painful; you find it hard to breathe; or you cough up blood.

At an appointment a GP may listen to your chest and arrange tests to find the cause, such as a chest X-ray.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: note that cough linked with fever, shortness of breath or chest pain needs different attention than a mild post-cold cough. A cough lasting beyond about three weeks is worth a GP visit. Not medical advice.

# Heavy periods

Heavy periods (menorrhagia) are common, and for some people they are normal. The NHS says treatment can help if they affect daily life.

You may have heavy periods if you: need to change a pad or tampon every 1 to 2 hours, or empty a menstrual cup more often than advised; need two types of product together; have periods lasting more than 7 days; pass clots larger than about 2.5 cm; bleed through to clothes or bedding; avoid daily activities such as exercise or take time off work because of your periods; or often feel tired or short of breath.

Why it happens: heaviness can be normal at different times, such as when periods first start, after pregnancy or approaching menopause. Sometimes it is linked to conditions of the womb or ovaries (such as fibroids, endometriosis, adenomyosis, pelvic inflammatory disease or the hormone condition now called PMOS), to conditions that make bleeding easier, or to some medicines and treatments. Rarely, it can be a sign of womb cancer.

When to see a clinician: see a GP if heavy periods affect your life or have gone on for some time, if you have severe pain with periods, if you bleed between periods or after sex, or if you have heavy periods with pain when peeing, pooing or having sex. Heavy bleeding with a lot of tiredness or breathlessness is also worth discussing because blood loss can cause low iron.

Treatment is not always needed. Where it is, a GP may discuss contraception-based options or other medicines that reduce bleeding, and other procedures are possible. Choice depends on the person's circumstances, including plans for pregnancy.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: persistent tiredness alongside very heavy periods is a reason to prompt a conversation with a clinician, not a conclusion. Do not recommend medicines. Soaking through protection very quickly with dizziness or fainting needs urgent medical help. Not medical advice.

# Menopause and perimenopause: common symptoms

Menopause is when a person has not had a period for 12 months, and perimenopause is the lead-up, when hormone levels change. The NHS stresses that it is different for everyone: some people have symptoms that strongly affect relationships, social life, family and work, while others have hardly any. Symptoms can also continue after menopause. Ethnic background can influence which symptoms occur and how severe or long-lasting they are; for example, women from a Black ethnic background are more likely to have severe, longer-lasting hot flushes.

Common symptoms:

- **Period changes**: often one of the first signs. Periods may come more or less often and be heavier or lighter before stopping.
- **Hot flushes and night sweats**: sudden heat or cold in the face, neck and chest, sweating, palpitations, anxiety or dizziness, lasting several minutes.
- **Sleep problems**: trouble falling or staying asleep, worse with night sweats.
- **Mood, memory and concentration**: mood swings, low mood, brain fog.
- **Weight gain**, often around the stomach and upper body.
- **Vaginal dryness, irritation or itching**, which can make sex painful, and more urinary tract infections or urgency.
- Others: palpitations, bone density loss, headaches, joint aches, hair thinning, dry skin, lower libido and mouth or gum problems.

When to see a clinician: contact a GP if you think you have symptoms and want to know your options; if you have a fast heartbeat; if you still have periods but bleeding is heavier than before; or if you have any vaginal bleeding after 12 months without a period. Early advice can reduce the effect on health, relationships and work.

Lifestyle that supports general wellbeing at this stage, drawn from other sources, includes regular activity, a balanced diet, good sleep habits and stress management.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: sleep and mood data may shift. Reflect without assuming the cause, and offer a clinician conversation. Not medical advice.

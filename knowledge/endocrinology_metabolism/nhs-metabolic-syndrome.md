# Metabolic syndrome: a cluster of risk factors

Metabolic syndrome is the name for a group of health problems that together raise the risk of type 2 diabetes and heart and blood vessel disease. The NHS notes it is different from the rare inherited metabolic disorders.

There are often no symptoms, so it is usually found through a blood test or check-up. Its cause is not fully known, but it is linked to insulin resistance (the body not responding properly to insulin) and to carrying extra fat around the tummy. It is more likely with obesity, a diet high in saturated fat, little exercise, smoking, heavy drinking, older age, and a Hispanic or South Asian background.

How it is checked: a clinician asks about personal and family health, checks BMI, waist and blood pressure, and arranges blood tests for glucose and blood fats such as cholesterol. Sometimes fasting is requested beforehand, in which case follow the clinic's instructions.

Treatment centres on lifestyle change; a clinician may also offer medicines for weight, insulin resistance, cholesterol or blood pressure. The page lists healthy habits: lose weight if overweight; eat less saturated fat and fewer meat and dairy products, with more fruit, vegetables and whole grains; do at least 150 minutes of moderate-to-intense activity a week across at least four or five days; reduce or stop smoking; and avoid long periods of sitting.

Possible complications: cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes, fatty liver disease, osteoarthritis, gout, some cancers, and sometimes reduced fertility.

When to see a clinician: see a GP if you think you are at risk of metabolic syndrome.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: movement, balanced meals, sleep and stress management are broadly supportive. It should not label anyone as having the syndrome or interpret lab results. Not medical advice.

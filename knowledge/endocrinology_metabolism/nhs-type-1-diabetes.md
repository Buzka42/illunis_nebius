# Type 1 diabetes: recognising the symptoms early

Type 1 diabetes is a condition in which the body cannot make insulin, so blood glucose rises too high. It usually starts in children and young adults but can happen at any age, and it is more likely with other autoimmune conditions or a family history of them.

The NHS describes the main symptoms with the "4 Ts": **toilet** (peeing more, especially at night), **thirsty** (feeling very thirsty and drinking more), **tired**, and **thinner** (losing weight without trying). Children may wet the bed or have heavier nappies. Others include blurred vision, breath that smells sweet or fruity, slow-healing cuts, and frequent infections such as thrush. Unlike type 2, symptoms develop quickly, over days or weeks.

Why it is urgent: untreated, type 1 diabetes can lead to diabetic ketoacidosis, a serious and potentially life-threatening condition.

What the page advises:

- If you think you or your child has type 1 diabetes, ask for an urgent GP appointment or contact NHS 111.
- Call emergency services or go to A&E if increased peeing, thirst and tiredness come with symptoms that are getting worse quickly, stomach pain, diarrhoea or being sick, sleepiness or confusion, or faster, deeper breathing than usual. These can be signs of ketoacidosis. Do not drive yourself; ask someone else or call an ambulance.

Diagnosis uses a finger-prick glucose test and sometimes a urine sample. If results suggest type 1 diabetes, the person is usually seen by a hospital specialist the same day to start treatment.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: if a user (or a parent) describes this cluster of symptoms, tell them to contact a clinician or urgent care promptly instead of tracking symptoms. Treatment is managed by a diabetes team and the assistant should not discuss insulin dosing. Not medical advice.

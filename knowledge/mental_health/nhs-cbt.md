# Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT): what it is and how to access it

CBT is a talking therapy in which a therapist helps a person change how they think and act. The NHS lists it as a recommended treatment for many mental health problems, including depression, anxiety (including social anxiety, phobias and obsessive compulsive disorder), post-traumatic stress disorder, eating disorders, bipolar disorder and personality disorders. It can also help with sleep problems such as insomnia, alcohol and drug problems, long-term pain, irritable bowel syndrome and chronic fatigue.

How to get it: CBT is available free on the NHS for many problems, and you do not need a formal diagnosis. Waiting times vary by area. For many problems, such as anxiety and depression, adults (18 or over, or 16 or over in some areas) can refer themselves to an NHS talking therapies service without seeing a GP. For some conditions, including eating disorders, bipolar disorder, personality disorders or psychosis, a GP referral is needed. CBT can also be arranged privately with an accredited therapist, or through an employer, university or local charity. The process is different for under-18s.

What to expect: after referral you have an assessment, in person, online or by phone, where a therapist asks about symptoms and goals and recommends the right kind of therapy, which may be CBT or something else. CBT takes several forms, such as guided self-help (a workbook or online course with a therapist's support) and one-to-one sessions. The therapist and the person work on how thoughts, feelings, body sensations and actions affect each other, and practise new ways of responding.

Self-help CBT techniques also exist, such as short video guides and worksheets, as part of NHS Every Mind Matters. These are general tools, not a substitute for therapy when someone is struggling.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: when a user describes ongoing worry, low mood or poor sleep, mention that talking therapy is available and often self-referral is possible. Not medical advice.

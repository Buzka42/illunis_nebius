# Generalised anxiety: persistent, hard-to-control worry

Generalised anxiety disorder (GAD) is a common mental health condition in which a person often feels very anxious about many different things. The main symptom, according to the NHS, is worry or stress that affects daily life and is difficult to control.

Other symptoms may include trouble sleeping, restlessness, irritability or poor concentration, getting tired easily, feeling tense, stomach problems, noticeable heartbeat (palpitations), light-headedness or dizziness, and low mood. Other anxiety conditions, such as panic disorder and phobias, can also occur, sometimes together.

Is it GAD? Most people feel anxious sometimes, which does not mean they have GAD. It is more likely if you feel anxious most of the time and it affects everyday life. The page directs people unsure to general advice about anxiety, fear and panic.

Getting help: see a GP if you think you might have GAD, or if you have a diagnosis and treatments are not helping. Adults 18 or over can refer themselves directly to an NHS talking therapies service without a GP. If you are pregnant or gave birth within the last 12 months, a midwife, doctor or health visitor can also help. A GP asks about worries and physical symptoms, and honesty about feelings helps with diagnosis. Treatment is chosen with a clinician and may include talking therapies and medicines.

Urgent help: the page links to urgent mental health support for anyone in crisis. If you are in immediate danger or thinking about ending your life, contact local emergency services or a crisis line now. In the UK, Samaritans answer at any time on 116 123.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: reflect observed patterns, such as poor sleep and tension across days, and offer calming practices from the breath and stress notes. It should not label anyone with GAD. Not medical advice.

# Anxiety, fear and panic: symptoms and things to try

Most people feel anxious or scared sometimes. The NHS notes that when it affects life there are self-help steps and support available.

Anxiety can affect the body, mind and behaviour. Physical: a faster or irregular heartbeat, light-headedness, headaches, chest pains, loss of appetite, stomach upset, sweating, breathlessness, shaking. Mental: tension, being unable to relax, worrying about past or future, tearfulness, poor sleep, trouble concentrating, fear of the worst, intrusive or obsessive thoughts. Behaviour: avoiding places or situations, struggling to enjoy leisure time or relationships, compulsive checking.

A panic attack is sudden, intense anxiety, with a racing heartbeat, faintness, feeling of losing control, sweating or trembling, shortness of breath, tingling in the fingers or lips, and nausea. It usually lasts 5 to 20 minutes and, though frightening, is not dangerous and should not cause harm. Because chest pain and breathlessness can also signal heart or lung problems, new or unexplained chest symptoms should be checked by a clinician.

Things to try: talk about feelings with someone you trust; regular exercise such as walking, swimming or yoga; regular healthy meals; calming breathing or meditation; and free audio guides. Avoid doing everything at once (set small targets), dwelling on things you cannot change, avoiding anxiety-provoking situations (build up exposure slowly), telling yourself you are alone, and using alcohol, cigarettes, gambling or drugs to cope.

When to see a clinician: see a GP if you are struggling to cope, self-help is not working, or you would like a referral. Adults can also self-refer to NHS talking therapies. If you are in immediate danger or thinking about ending your life, contact local emergency services or a crisis line now. In the UK, Samaritans answer at any time on 116 123.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: offer a short breathing practice or grounding step and gentle encouragement toward support. Not medical advice.

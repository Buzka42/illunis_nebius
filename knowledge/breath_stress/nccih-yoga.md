# Yoga for stress and general wellbeing

The US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health describes yoga as an ancient practice rooted in Indian philosophy that began as spiritual and is now popular for physical and mental wellbeing. In the US it usually combines postures (asanas), breathing techniques (pranayama) and meditation. Styles range from gentle to physically demanding, which makes research harder to compare. Yoga, tai chi and qigong are sometimes grouped as "meditative movement".

What research suggests yoga may do: improve general wellness by easing stress and improving mental and emotional health, sleep and balance; ease neck pain, migraine or tension-type headaches and knee osteoarthritis pain, with a small possible benefit for low-back pain; support weight loss; help people quit smoking; help manage anxiety or depression symptoms; ease menopause symptoms; and help people with chronic diseases manage symptoms. These are possible benefits, not guarantees.

On stress specifically: a 2020 review of 12 recent studies (672 healthy adults) found that every study showed a benefit on perceived stress, and an earlier review of 17 studies found 12 with improvements in measures related to stress. A 2018 review of 14 studies found most reported benefits for resilience or general mental wellbeing.

Safety: the page calls yoga generally safe for healthy people when done properly with a qualified instructor. Injuries can still happen. The most common are sprains and strains, often at the knee or lower leg, and serious injuries are rare. The injury risk is lower than for higher-impact activity, and older adults may need to take particular care. People with a health condition, pregnancy or pain should talk to a clinician first.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: yoga is one option on a menu that also includes breathing, a walk or stretching. Suggest gentle styles for beginners and stress the importance of stopping if something hurts. Injuries or ongoing pain are for a clinician, not for the assistant.

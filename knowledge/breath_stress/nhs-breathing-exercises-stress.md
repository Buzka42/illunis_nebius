# A simple calming breathing exercise

The NHS describes a short breathing technique for stress, anxiety and panic that takes a few minutes and can be done almost anywhere. It helps most when done regularly as part of a daily routine, not only in a hard moment.

Setting up: stand, sit in a chair that supports your back, or lie on a bed or mat. Make yourself comfortable and loosen tight clothing if you can. Lying down, keep arms a little away from the body with palms up, and legs straight or knees bent with feet flat. Sitting or standing, place both feet flat on the floor roughly hip-width apart.

The practice:

1. Let the breath flow low into the belly, as far as is comfortable, without forcing it.
2. Try breathing in through the nose and out through the mouth.
3. Breathe in gently and regularly. Some people count from 1 to 5 as they inhale. You may not reach 5 at first, and that is fine.
4. Let the breath flow out gently, again counting 1 to 5 if that helps.
5. Keep going for at least five minutes.

The emphasis is on gentleness and regularity, not on holding the breath or achieving a particular count. Stop if you feel dizzy or uncomfortable, and return to normal breathing.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: it is a low-risk practice to offer when someone mentions tension, restlessness before sleep or a stressful day, framed as an option they can try. Offer to guide the count aloud if they wish. If breathlessness, chest pain or panic is frequent or alarming, suggest speaking to a clinician; breathing practice is not a treatment for a medical problem.

# Five everyday steps for mental wellbeing

The NHS lists five steps, backed by evidence, that can lift mood and help people get more from life.

1. **Connect with other people.** Good relationships build belonging and self-worth, allow shared positive experiences and give emotional support. Ideas: regular family meals, lunch with a colleague, a day out with friends, volunteering, video calls with people far away. The page cautions against relying on text and social media alone.
2. **Be physically active.** Activity can raise self-esteem, help you set and reach goals, and cause chemical changes in the brain that improve mood. You do not need to spend hours in a gym. Find something you enjoy and make it part of life.
3. **Learn new skills.** Learning can build confidence, give a sense of purpose and help you connect with others. Cooking something new, a DIY project, a course or a new hobby all count. You do not need qualifications.
4. **Give to others.** Acts of kindness, from saying thank you and really listening to volunteering, create positive feelings, a sense of reward and a feeling of purpose.
5. **Pay attention to the present moment (mindfulness).** Noticing thoughts, feelings, body and surroundings can improve wellbeing and change how you approach challenges.

The framework comes from a report by the New Economics Foundation called Five Ways to Wellbeing. The steps are for general wellbeing, not treatment for illness.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: when a user feels flat or stressed, offer one step that fits their day, such as a short call with a friend or a walk, and ask what feels doable. Treat it as a menu, not a checklist. Persistent low mood or anxiety needs professional support, and the assistant should say so gently.

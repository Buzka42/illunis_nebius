# Bloating: common, usually gas

Bloating is when the tummy feels full, bigger than usual or uncomfortable, sometimes with rumbling, pain or more wind. It is very common. The most frequent reason is gas in the gut, from certain foods and drinks (some vegetables, fizzy drinks) or from swallowing air while eating. It can also come with digestive problems such as constipation, a food intolerance, coeliac disease or irritable bowel syndrome, and some people feel bloated around their period. The NHS adds that bloating that does not go away can occasionally be a sign of something more serious, including ovarian cancer.

Ideas to reduce it: regular exercise, chewing with the mouth closed, drinking plenty of water, eating soluble fibre such as oats or linseed if constipated, smaller and more frequent meals, and gentle tummy massage from right to left. Cut back on fizzy drinks, alcohol, caffeine, gas-producing foods such as cabbage, beans and lentils, large late-night meals, and heavily processed, sugary, spicy or fatty food. Avoid foods you know you are intolerant to.

When to see a clinician: see a GP if you are bloated regularly, diet changes have not helped, or you have bloating with unintentional weight loss or blood in your poo. Ask for an urgent GP appointment or call NHS 111 if bloating comes with vomiting, diarrhoea or constipation, a stomach ache, a high temperature or shivers, a swelling or lump in the tummy, or if you cannot pee, poo or pass wind. Call emergency services or go to A&E if bloating with a swollen stomach comes with severe, sudden stomach pain, vomiting blood or vomit like ground coffee, or severe difficulty breathing.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: a simple food and symptom diary can help the user spot patterns to share with a clinician. Persistent bloating deserves a check, not just diet tweaks. Not medical advice.

# Heartburn and acid reflux

Heartburn is a burning feeling in the middle of the chest caused by stomach acid travelling up toward the throat (acid reflux). When it keeps happening it can be called gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD). Along with heartburn there may be a sour taste, a recurring cough or hiccups, a hoarse voice, bad breath, bloating and nausea. Symptoms are often worse after eating, lying down or bending over.

Many people get it from time to time with no clear reason. It can be caused or worsened by coffee, tomatoes, alcohol, chocolate and fatty or spicy foods; being overweight; smoking; pregnancy; stress; hormones such as progesterone and oestrogen; some medicines such as anti-inflammatory painkillers; a hiatus hernia; a stomach ulcer; or a bacterial stomach infection.

Lifestyle steps the NHS lists: eat smaller, more frequent meals; try to lose weight if overweight; find ways to relax; avoid trigger foods; do not eat within 3 or 4 hours of bed; avoid tight waistbands; do not smoke; limit alcohol; and do not stop any prescribed medicine without speaking to a doctor. Raising the head of the bed by about 10 to 20 cm with blocks can help, whereas piling up pillows may increase pressure on the belly and worsen symptoms. A pharmacist can suggest short-term remedies, but these are not a cure and should not be used regularly over long periods.

When to see a clinician: see a GP if lifestyle changes and pharmacy products are not helping, if you have heartburn most days, or if you also have food sticking in your throat, frequent vomiting or unexplained weight loss. A GP can look for other causes. Contact a GP or NHS 111 if symptoms are getting worse.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: late meals and alcohol show up in the user's diary as plain patterns to reflect back. Chest pain that spreads, or comes with sweating or breathlessness, needs emergency help. Not medical advice.

# Diarrhoea and vomiting: fluids first, and warning signs

Diarrhoea and vomiting are common at any age and are often caused by a stomach bug. The NHS advises that they usually settle at home, and the most important thing is to drink plenty of fluids to avoid dehydration. Diarrhoea usually stops within 5 to 7 days and vomiting within 1 to 2 days.

Home care: stay home and rest, drink lots of fluids such as water or squash in small sips if you feel sick, eat when you can (avoid fatty or spicy food), and avoid fruit juice and fizzy drinks, which can make diarrhoea worse. For babies, keep feeding and give small feeds more often; formula must not be made weaker.

Stop it spreading: stay off school or work until you have been free of sickness and diarrhoea for at least 2 days; wash hands with soap and water frequently; wash soiled clothes and bedding separately on a hot wash; clean toilet surfaces daily; do not prepare food for others if possible; do not share towels or utensils; and avoid swimming pools until 48 hours after symptoms stop.

Speak to a pharmacist if you or a child over 5 show dehydration signs such as dark smelly pee or peeing less, if you are older or have a weakened immune system or other conditions that raise the risk of dehydration, or if you need relief for a few hours.

Call NHS 111 if you are worried about a baby under 12 months, a child stops feeding or has dehydration signs, you cannot keep fluids down, there is bloody diarrhoea or bleeding from the bottom, or diarrhoea lasts over 7 days or vomiting over 2 days.

Call emergency services or go to A&E for vomiting blood or vomit like ground coffee, green vomit in adults, possible swallowed poison, stiff neck with pain from bright lights, sudden severe headache or tummy ache, blue, grey or pale skin or lips, severe breathing difficulty, or confusion.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: stick to hydration, rest and warning signs. Not medical advice.

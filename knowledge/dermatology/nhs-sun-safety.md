# Sun safety and sunscreen

The NHS says sunburn increases your risk of skin cancer, and you can burn in the UK even when it is cloudy. There is no safe or healthy way to get a tan, because a tan does not protect the skin from the sun's harmful effects. The aim is to balance sun protection against getting enough vitamin D from sunlight.

Core tips: spend time in the shade when the sun is strongest (in the UK, 11am to 3pm from March to October); check the UV index on a weather app and consider protecting skin when it is 3 or higher; never burn; cover up with suitable clothing, a wide-brimmed hat and sunglasses; take extra care with children; keep babies under six months out of direct sunlight; and use sunscreen of at least factor 30, applied generously and reapplied often. Sunscreen should not be your only protection.

Choosing a product: look for a sun protection factor (SPF) of at least 30 for UVB and at least a 4-star UVA rating, which can also be shown as the UVA-in-a-circle mark. Check the expiry date and the open-jar symbol showing how many months it lasts once opened.

Applying it: most people use too little. For the whole body an adult should apply a generous amount, and if you will be out long enough to burn, apply it twice, once 30 minutes before going out and again just before. Cover all exposed skin including face, neck and ears. Reapply straight after swimming, towel drying or sweating, even with water-resistant products, and about every two hours. Do not stay out longer than you would without sunscreen.

Water: swimming washes sunscreen off, the cooling effect hides burning, and water reflects UV, increasing exposure.

Children have more sensitive skin, and repeated damage can raise skin cancer risk later in life.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: a reminder about UV, hats and shade on bright days fits the outdoor-activity context. Questions about skin changes or moles go to a clinician. Not medical advice.

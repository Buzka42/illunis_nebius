# Sunburn: easing it and knowing when it is serious

Sunburn is skin damage from too much sun. The NHS says you can treat it yourself and it usually gets better within about 7 days. Skin may feel hot, sore or painful and may flake or peel a few days later, and blister if severe. On white skin it is usually red or pink; on black or brown skin you may not notice a colour change.

Easing it at home: get out of the sun as soon as possible; cool the skin with a cool shower, bath or damp towel (take care not to over-chill a baby or young child); apply aftersun or an unperfumed moisturiser; avoid alcohol and drink plenty of water to cool down and prevent dehydration; ask a pharmacist about pain relief; and keep burnt skin covered from direct sun until healed.

Avoid: petroleum jelly on sunburn; ice or ice packs on the skin; popping blisters; scratching or removing peeling skin; and tight-fitting clothes over burnt skin. A pharmacist can advise on treatments and whether to see a GP.

Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 if after sun exposure your skin is blistered or swollen; your temperature is very high or you feel hot, cold or shivery; you feel very tired, dizzy or sick; you have a headache or muscle cramps; or a baby or young child is sunburnt. Severe sunburn can lead to heat exhaustion and heatstroke, which can be very serious.

Prevention: shade, covering clothing, a hat, sunglasses and sunscreen, as covered in the sun safety note. Repeated sunburn raises long-term skin cancer risk.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: remind users that heat illness symptoms such as dizziness, headache and nausea after sun exposure need prompt attention, not waiting. Not medical advice.

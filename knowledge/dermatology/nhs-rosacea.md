# Rosacea: flushing and sensitive facial skin

Rosacea is a long-term skin condition that mainly affects the face. The NHS says it is more common in women and people with lighter skin, though symptoms can be worse in men. Treatment can help with symptoms, but it cannot be cured, and it can get worse if untreated.

First signs: redness (blushing) across the nose, cheeks, forehead, chin, neck and chest that comes and goes, usually lasting minutes, and may feel warm, hot or painful; and a burning or stinging feeling when using water or skincare products. Redness can be harder to see on brown or black skin. As it progresses, the cheeks, nose and forehead may be red all the time.

See a GP if you have symptoms of rosacea. A GP may suggest prescription creams or gels, or a long course of antibiotics, and may refer to a specialist where needed.

Urgent: ask for an urgent GP appointment or call NHS 111 if you have rosacea and your eye is painful, your vision is blurred, you are sensitive to light, your eye is red or it feels gritty. These could be signs of keratitis, which can be serious if not treated urgently.

General notes: many people find that certain things make flushing worse, such as heat, spicy food, alcohol and stress, and noting personal triggers can help in discussions with a clinician. Gentle skincare and sun protection are commonly advised, since sunlight can aggravate redness.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: a user who mentions recurring facial flushing can be encouraged to see a GP, and to track possible triggers in a diary. The assistant should not say which skin condition someone has. Not medical advice.

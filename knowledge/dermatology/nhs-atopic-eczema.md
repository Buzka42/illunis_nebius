# Atopic eczema: itchy, dry skin that flares

Atopic eczema (atopic dermatitis) is a common skin condition that causes itchy skin. It affects all ages but is most common in young children, usually starting in babies and young children and often improving with age. It cannot be cured, but treatment can help manage symptoms.

What it looks like: skin that is itchy, dry, cracked, crusty, scaly or thickened; colours vary with skin tone, and it may be red, white, purple, grey, lighter or darker than the surrounding skin; it may blister or bleed. It is common on the elbows, knees and hands, and on the face in babies and toddlers. There are flare-ups and calmer periods.

Triggers can include soap, washing detergent, some fabrics, pets, pollen, house-dust mites, certain foods and stress, though the cause is not always known.

General self-care from the NHS: avoid things that make it worse; apply moisturising treatments (emollients) as often as possible, at least twice a day, and keep going even when skin improves; wash with an emollient instead of soap; keep nails short; try rubbing gently instead of scratching; do not share emollients; use a spoon or pump instead of dipping fingers into a pot; and do not smoke or go near naked flames if using an emollient, because it can make fabrics catch fire more easily.

See a GP if you or your child have symptoms or treatments are not helping.

Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 if eczema is blistered, crusty, leaking fluid or has pus-filled spots; is painful, swollen or warm; suddenly gets worse or bigger; or you have a high temperature or feel generally unwell. These can be signs of infection or of a more serious condition called eczema herpeticum.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: sleep disturbed by itching and stress flares can be reflected back as patterns. Do not recommend creams or steroids. Not medical advice.

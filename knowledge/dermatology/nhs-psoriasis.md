# Psoriasis

Psoriasis is a common skin condition causing itchy and painful patches of dry, flaky skin. The NHS says it cannot be cured but treatments can help manage it.

The main symptom is dry, itchy, sore, flaky patches that look like silvery-white scales. Patches are usually 1 to 2 cm, oval or irregular; on white skin they are pink or red, and on brown or black skin they look grey and can leave dark marks afterwards. Most people have a few patches, others have large areas. It is most often found on the scalp and behind the ears, chest, tummy and back, bottom, forearms and elbows, and lower legs and knees, and can occur in body folds and fingernails (where dents and pits may appear). It can start at any age, often between 15 and 25 or 50 and 60, and comes in flare-ups and calmer periods.

See a GP if you have symptoms of psoriasis; if treatment is not working or is causing side effects; or if you have psoriasis and joint pain, stiffness and swelling that keeps coming back.

Call emergency services or go to A&E if your skin quickly turns red with white pus-filled bumps that join together; patches on the feet turn yellow-brown and fill with pus; or fingers and toes have red, scaly pus-filled bumps on the tips. These can be signs of a more serious condition or an infection, and may come with a fast heartbeat, high temperature or shivers. Do not drive yourself.

General wellbeing: stress, skin injury and some medicines can trigger flare-ups for some people, and a clinician can discuss this. Sleep and mood may be affected when skin is sore or itchy.

How a wellbeing assistant can use this: acknowledge flare-ups as a pattern and encourage regular follow-up with a clinician. Do not recommend creams or specific treatments. Not medical advice.
